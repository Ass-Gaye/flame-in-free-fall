This investigation particularly focused on the origin of sidebranches that occur when columnar solidification patterns transform from cellular to dendritic as a function of thermal gradient and solidification velocity and study the potential for the formation of an intermediate multiplet pattern. The dynamics and stability of cellular and dendritic growth patterns, and the relationship between the tip geometry and growth pattern were also to be investigated as well. This intent of this investigation was to directly address outstanding issues that remain open in the understanding of complex dendritic microstructures. Approach The DSI-R investigation utilized a Bridgman type furnace and an experimental cartridge containing the organic transparent alloy of succinonitrile (SCN) - camphor. The nominal concentration was 0.46 wt% camphor. A single crystalline solid seed with a 100 direction parallel to the pulling axial direction is kept unmelted during all experiments. Solidification is performed by pulling the experimental cartridge containing the alloy inside a thermal gradient G at a pulling rate V. The G and V were the primary variable for each experimental run. Images obtained by the direct axial observation, providing top-view interface images, and transverse observations, providing side-view interface images, were analyzed. Hypothesis Bridgman solidification of transparent binary alloys in a diffusion controlled growth regime can yield benchmark data required for establishing the detailed dynamics of interface pattern selection. The ''SPAtiotemporal evolution of three-dimensional DEndritic array Structures'' (SPADES) investigation conducted during the reflight of the Directional Solidification Insert (DSI-R) campaign targeted the columnar dendritic growth regime to quantitatively establish the fundamental physics controlling the spatiotemporal organization of the secondary sidebranch structure and its interaction with the array structure of primary branches under directional solidification conditions. SPADES focused on the origin of sidebranches that occur when columnar solidification patterns transform from cellular to dendritic as a function of thermal gradient and solidification velocity. The “Effects of Curvature and Unsteady Dynamics on Alloy Solidification Patterns” (CUSP) investigation extended the DSIP and SPADES project into a new direction that addressed the critical effects of the macroscopic curvature of the solidification front and the unsteady dynamics that occurs in response to changes of growth conditions or that is associated with side-branching. A weak macroscopic interface curvature can have a major effect on both the array pattern evolution and grain structure. The convex and concave interfaces lead to a continuous increase and decrease of the average primary spacing, respectively, and it only attains a stationary value for a flat interface. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits By providing a reduced gravity environment where fluid convection is suppressed, space enables the direct experimental observation of the solid-liquid interface dynamics during the solidification process under long-duration and purely diffusive conditions. The acquired benchmark data will make it uniquely possible to critically test computational models of alloy microstructures by quantitative comparisons of modeling predictions and experimental observations, thereby contributing to the development of Integrated Computational Materials and Engineering (ICME) tools for both space and earth applications. Those tools are expected to “shorten development time, optimize designs and reduce costs relative to more traditional strategies involving building prototypes, testing and redesigning philosophies. In addition, understanding alloy solidification in the microgravity environment of space is important to improve in-space processing (manufacturing, joining, and welding) of structural materials that will be used for future space exploration.
