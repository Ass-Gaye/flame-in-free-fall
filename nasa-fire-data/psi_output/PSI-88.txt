The hydrodynamics consists of the pressure drop inside the packed bed and the liquid hold-up. The different regimes are the results of different liquid and gas flow rates, and different initial conditions embodied by gas and liquid pre-flows or flushes prior to beginning of the runs. Another objective of the experiment was to understand transient flow rates and the hysteresis in the pressure drop as a function of the flow rates. Approach The PBRE-2 investigations employed a systematic and comprehensive approach to study the hydrodynamics of two-phase flow in packed bed reactors under microgravity conditions. The approach can be summarized in the following key steps: 1. Ground-Based Research and Model Development: Prior to the spaceflight experiments, extensive ground-based research was conducted to develop theoretical models and establish a foundation for understanding two-phase flow in packed beds. This included: Literature review: A thorough review of existing literature on single-phase and two-phase flow through porous media, focusing on pressure drop, flow regimes, and the influence of various parameters. Development of hydrodynamic models: Formulation of mathematical models, such as the modified Ergun equation, to predict pressure drop and flow regime transitions in microgravity. Ground-based experiments: Conducting experiments in reduced gravity environments, such as parabolic flights and drop towers, to gather preliminary data and validate initial model predictions. 2. Design and Development of Spaceflight Experiment: A dedicated experimental facility was designed and developed for operation on the ISS. This involved: Test facility design: Engineering a flow control system, test section, and data acquisition system capable of operating reliably in microgravity and meeting the specific requirements of the investigation. Definition of test matrix: Developing a comprehensive test matrix encompassing a wide range of gas and liquid flow rates, pre-flow conditions, and transient flow scenarios. 3. Execution of Spaceflight Experiments: The PBRE-2 experiments were conducted on the ISS, following the defined test matrix and procedures. This involved: Preflow procedures: Implementing the chosen pre-flow treatment (liquid flush or gas flush) before each test run. Flow control: Precisely controlling and varying the gas and liquid flow rates according to the test matrix. Data acquisition: Collecting data from pressure sensors, temperature sensors, and high-speed cameras throughout each test run. Downlinking data: Transmitting the acquired data to the ground for analysis. 4. Data Analysis and Interpretation: The downlinked data was meticulously analyzed to: Characterize flow regimes: Identify and classify the different flow regimes observed in the experiments based on pressure traces and video recordings. Evaluate pressure drop: Analyze the pressure drop data and compare it with model predictions to assess the validity and accuracy of the models. Investigate flow regime transitions: Determine the conditions and mechanisms leading to transitions between different flow regimes. Assess the influence of packing properties and pre-flow conditions: Analyze the impact of packing material wettability, size, and pre-flow treatments on the flow behavior and pressure drop. 5. Dissemination of Results: The findings of the PBRE-2 investigations were disseminated through: Peer-reviewed publications: Publishing research papers in scientific journals to share the results with the broader scientific community. Presentations at conferences: Presenting the research findings at relevant scientific conferences and workshops. Hypothesis It is hypothesized that in microgravity two-phase packed bed flows, pressure drop will be a function of surface tension (captured by a modified Ergun equation), packing wettability and size, and pre-flow conditions. Furthermore, bubble removal will depend on bubble volume and liquid flow rate, and distinct flow regimes will emerge, with transitions governed by specific gas and liquid flow rate combinations. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Fundamental understanding the impact of reduced or zero gravity on the hydrodynamics (pressure drop, as well as distribution and holdup of each phase) is relevant to systems that involve two-phase flow such as life support systems, fuel cells, in-situ resource utilization, heat pipes, materials processing, production of pharmaceutical-grade water, and transport of nutrients in soils. Design and operation of many chemical and biological processing systems require this fundamental understanding of two-phase gas-liquid flows through porous media because many of these space-based systems or unit operations involve gas-liquid flows through porous media. Some examples of current space systems involving two-phase flow through fixed porous media include the Volatile Removal Assembly (VRA), the Integrated Advanced Water Recovery System (AWRS), and IntraVenous Water GENeration system (IVGEN). The ability to predict flow regimes and corresponding pressure drop in a particular system involving two-phase flow is crucial to the design of the fluid system and the pumping requirements.
