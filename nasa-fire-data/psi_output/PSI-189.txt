Develop and validate computational models for gas-liquid two-phase flow in porous media under microgravity using the PBRE (Packed Bed Reactor Experiment) flight data available in Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI). 2. Understand capillary-dominated fluid dynamics in low-gravity environments where traditional gravity-driven models fail. 3. Advance CFD tools to support the design and scale-up of life support and thermal control systems for spacecraft, space stations, and lunar/Martian habitats. Approach In this grant, our approach combined high-fidelity simulations with reduced-order modeling to better understand and predict two-phase gas-liquid flows through packed bed reactors in microgravity. We began by developing interface-resolved CFD simulations using the Volume of Fluid (VOF) method to capture detailed bubble dynamics in a representative volume of the reactor. These simulations were validated using PBRE imagery and pressure data available in PSI. To address scale-up and improve computational efficiency, we also developed a two-fluid model (TFM), which treats the gas and liquid phases as interpenetrating continua rather than tracking individual interfaces. We calibrated the drag closure in this model using PBRE data, allowing us to predict pressure drop behavior across different flow regimes. By combining detailed CFD with a validated TFM, we created a versatile modeling framework that supports the design of life support and thermal systems for future space missions. Purdue University Research Repository. doi:10.4231/VHB3-EN13. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits In space, fluid behavior becomes unpredictable. This grant developed and validated advanced computational models using real data from microgravity experiments to improve our understanding of how gas and liquid mixtures flow through packed bed reactors in space systems. This research can enable the design of more reliable and scalable systems for future missions to the Moon and Mars. On Earth, the same fluid dynamics apply to industries like chemical processing, wastewater treatment, and energy systems. The resulting models and insights from this research can improve efficiency and reduce costs in these applications.
