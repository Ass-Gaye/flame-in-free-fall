The BCAT-6 investigation had four primary objectives corresponding to specific samples: Phase Separation (BCAT-6-Phase Seperation): to study how gas and liquid separate and join together in space. Colloidal liquids, which have tiny particles suspended throughout, are used to study how the two different phases interact. This research gives fundamental insight into the nature of supercritical fluids--fluids having both liquid and gas properties. This information could be used to develop colloidal materials that last longer. Colloidal Disks (BCAT-6-Colloidal Disks): to improve understanding of properties of liquid crystals. Polystyrene - Deoxyribonucleic Acid (BCAT-6-PS-DNA): to use DNA as a type of molecular glue to specifically stick small particles together. The experiment uses microscopic polymer beads in solution (colloids) that have been coated with DNA. The DNA only binds to its complement and hence keeps specific particles together to form designer crystals. Seeded Growth (BCAT-6-Seeded Growth): to build on previous research looking at how dense groups of particles may be coaxed to form crystal structures when much larger “seed” particles are added. Some materials may consist of large individual crystals, or groups of many smaller crystals organized in a larger structure. Knowing when and how either type of crystal will form gives insight into how to control crystal growth which is important in many industrial processes. Approach BCAT is implemented in a bench-top investigation which is set up on the Maintenance Work Area (MWA) on ISS. The samples are contained within a Fluids Module (FM) which held 10 samples in cuvettes in individual wells. The samples were manually homogenized using a stir bar (a Teflon coated mixing magnet) inside each cuvette. Samples are mixed, examined and imaged using EarthKAM, to document the separation of colloid and liquid components and the formation of colloidal crystals over time. Hypothesis Colloidal particles would exhibit behaviors analogous to atoms, allowing the study of phase transitions, crystallization, and self-organization without the interference of sedimentation and convection present on Earth. This investigation hypothesizes that by taking advantage of microgravity and avoiding sedimentation binary alloys with very different materials and sizes and by addition of polymers particles to induce attractive forces can lead to exciting and promising form of colloidal engineering to synthesize a new class of materials. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Colloidal phase separation research in microgravity supports both space and Earth applications, advancing technologies such as life-support systems, rocket fuels, and nanoparticle behavior in spacecraft. It enhances liquid crystals for astronaut helmets and energy-efficient displays while enabling space-based production of larger, higher-quality crystals. On Earth, studies of colloidal materials and liquid crystal models improve product shelf life, tunable displays, and molecular nanotechnology. Innovations include DNA-programmed crystals for advanced diagnostics, industrial materials, and next-generation electronics, benefiting industries like medicine, plastics, and consumer devices.
