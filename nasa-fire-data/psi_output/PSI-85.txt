Specific objectives included: - Single bubble dynamics (nucleation, growth, and departure) under microgravity conditions under constant wall temperature. - Effect of liquid subcooling, wall superheat, and system pressure on single bubble dynamics. - Heat flux variation on the heated surface during single bubble evolution. - Lateral merger of bubbles formed at neighboring sites and mechanism of vapor removal from the surface. Approach The research utilized a systematic and comprehensive approach to understand the complexities of nucleate pool boiling in a microgravity environment. The approach was characterized by a strategic ''building block'' strategy, progressing from simpler to more complex scenarios, enabling a gradual and insightful understanding of the underlying physics. Here's a detailed breakdown of the investigation approach: 1. Single Bubble Dynamics: The research commenced by focusing on the behavior of a single bubble nucleating from a pre-fabricated cavity on the aluminum wafer. This fundamental building block allowed for a detailed examination of bubble growth, departure (if any), and the associated heat transfer processes in microgravity conditions. 2. Multiple Bubble Mergers: Building upon the knowledge gained from single bubble studies, the investigation then explored the interactions of multiple bubbles. Experiments were conducted to study the merger of bubbles nucleating from different cavities. This stage investigated how bubbles merge in the lateral direction, the mechanisms of vapor removal from the heated surface after merger, and the impact of these interactions on the overall heat transfer rates. 3. Integral Nucleate Boiling: The investigation then broadened its scope by conducting experiments with boiling occurring over the entire heater surface. This aimed to obtain the boiling curve, which depicts the relationship between heat flux and wall superheat, under microgravity conditions. This provided a macroscopic view of nucleate pool boiling, considering the collective behavior of numerous bubbles. 4. Numerical Simulations: The experimental work was tightly coupled with complete numerical simulations of the boiling process. These simulations employed advanced computational fluid dynamics (CFD) techniques to capture the intricate details of bubble dynamics and heat transfer. The simulations accounted for a range of factors including time-varying system pressure and temperatures, the presence of dissolved gas in the liquid, surface tension effects, and the influence of microlayer evaporation. The numerical model was rigorously validated against the experimental data obtained in the NPBX experiments. 5. Model Development: The ultimate goal of the research was to develop a mechanistic model for nucleate pool boiling heat transfer under microgravity conditions. This model, based on the physical understanding derived from experimental observations and validated numerical simulations, aimed to provide a reliable tool for predicting boiling behavior across a wide range of parameters and conditions relevant to space applications. Hypothesis In microgravity conditions, where buoyancy forces become negligible, it is expected that forces arising from the inertia of the liquid, evaporation, and condensation at the vapor-liquid interface, disjoining pressure, capillary pressure gradient, and the resulting flow field will become more dominant. The study aimed to understand and quantify the effect of these forces and identify the conditions under which quasi-static or steady-state boiling is possible under prolonged microgravity duration. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits While the primary motivation for this investigation was to advance our understanding of boiling heat transfer in the context of space applications, the knowledge gained has significant implications for Earth-based technologies and scientific understanding as well. Here are some key potential impacts and benefits: 1. Improved Understanding of Boiling at Earth Gravity: By studying boiling in a microgravity environment where buoyancy forces are negligible, researchers can gain a clearer understanding of the interplay of other forces and phenomena that influence boiling behavior. This knowledge can, in turn, enhance our comprehension of boiling processes under Earth's gravity, where buoyancy often masks the effects of these more subtle factors. 2. Development of More Efficient Heat Transfer Systems: The insights gained from this research can contribute to the development of more efficient boiling heat transfer systems for a wide range of applications on Earth. Enhanced efficiency translates to reduced energy consumption, lower operational costs, and a reduced environmental footprint. 3. Advancements in Miniaturization of Electronics: As electronic devices continue to shrink in size, effective thermal management becomes increasingly critical. Understanding boiling in microgravity is crucial for developing innovative cooling solutions for miniaturized electronics, as these devices often operate at high heat fluxes and face challenges in dissipating heat effectively. 4. New Insights into Fluid Physics: This research offers valuable insights into fundamental fluid physics phenomena, including the behavior of bubbles, interfacial phenomena, and the dynamics of multiphase flows. This knowledge can lead to advancements in diverse fields, such as materials science, biomedical engineering, and environmental science. Contact(s) Vijay K.
