Approach • The anomalous nucleation events of interest are hypothesized to be caused by cavitation in the melt. Experiments replicated the conditions in which the nucleation events were previously observed and explored similar experimental conditions. • Magnetohydrodynamic modeling of fluid flow is to be used to determine the flow conditions during ISS-EML experiments. Relevant parameters include fluid velocity, shear rates, turbulence intensity, and their distribution. Such data is critical for control of experiments and input to other theoretical and modeling efforts. Hypothesis • Dynamic nucleation is an effect of the fluid flow and not reliant on classical nucleation theory. • Thermophysical property measurements may require appropriate flow conditions, which should be quantified • Flow conditions may affect solidification and nucleation behavior • Oxygen may influence surface tension measurements in metallic melts Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Containerless processing provides access to the deeply undercooled region of metallic melts. Using this unique processing environement we are able to investigate fundamental solidification behaivor and meausure thermophysical properties of metallic melts including density, surface tension, viscosity, thermal expansion, heat capacity, thermal conductivity, and electrical conductivity. More specifically, impacts and benefits are broken out by the 3 associated flight experiments. In PARSEC, the effect of fluid flow on the solidification path of certain alloys is investigated. Control of the solidification path would enable tailoring of the microstructure and properties of metal parts for specific applications. In ThermoLAB, the thermophysical properties of high-temperature materials is investigated. Many of these materials are used commercially. A better understanding of the physical properties will allow more efficient and more reliable production of metallic parts using these alloys. Other alloys are chosen because of scientific interest in their properties, which may illuminate fundamental physics related to the formation of bulk metallic glasses and quasicrystals. The nucleation and growth of quasicrystals, and the effect of atomic-scale order on the macroscopic properties of these alloys is investigated in ICOPROSOL. This fundamental investigation may improve our ability to tailor the microstructure of metals for commercial applications.
