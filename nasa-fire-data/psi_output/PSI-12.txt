This sheds light on how the antioxidant protein helps protect the human body from oxidizing radiation and oxides created as a byproduct of metabolism. For best results, the analysis technique requires large crystals with minimal imperfections, which are more easily produced in the microgravity environment of the space station. Approach The Growth of Large, Perfect Protein Crystals for Neutron Crystallography investigation grows perfect, large-volume crystal suitable for neutron macromolecular crystallography of large unit cell protein crystals in microgravity. The protein of study in this investigation is human manganese superoxide dismutase (MnSOD or SOD2), which has an enzymatic mechanism that relies on a proton shuttling pattern that has yet to be fully elucidated. Neutron macromolecular crystallography allows for visualization of protons that cannot be achieved by XRay Diffraction or Cryo-EM, but requires large-volume crystals minimal imperfection for best results. Gravity has been implicated as a cause of defects in crystals grown on Earth. The marriage of the liquid-liquid capillary diffusion crystal growth technique to the microgravity environment aboard the International Space Station (ISS) allows for the growth of multiple greater than 1 mm3 crystals with very few defects. Please refer to AccessingPerfectCrystalData.pdf under the Science Documents section. Hypothesis In 2001, the Borgstahl laboratory successfully grew large crystals of SOD using microgravity conditions on the International Space Station (ISS). Using existing crystallization hardware that uses the Granada Crystallization Boxes (GCB) for capillary counter diffusion protocols will be used to achieve microgravity crystallization results for SOD. The principal outcome will be to identify the role of hydrogen atoms in enzymatic activity, discern superoxide from peroxide, and water from hydroxide ion by their protonation state and decipher a structure-based mechanism for human MnSOD more precisely than from previous X-ray crystallographic models determined from Earth-grown crystals. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Manganese superoxide dismutase (MnSOD) is implicated in a number of human diseases, including non-Hodgkins lymphoma, lung cancer and colorectal cancer. Understanding how it works is an important step toward developing better prevention and treatment methods for such diseases. In space application, MnSOD could help researchers protect astronauts on future long-term missions. Astronauts receive significantly more exposure to oxidizing radiation than people on Earth, and on long journeys such as to Mars may receive dangerous doses that create Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS). These can damage DNA and other biomolecules, causing diseases such as cancer. The manganese superoxide dismutase (MnSOD) enzyme converts ROS into molecular oxygen and hydrogen peroxide, which other enzymes can then break down. Studies on MnSOD can help researchers can develop artificial techniques to reduce ROS and help protect astronauts on future long-term missions.
