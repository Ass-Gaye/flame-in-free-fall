Investigations of nucleation of crystal phases from supercooled (metastable) liquids as a function of stirring (fluid-flow) conditions. Systematic studies of this under terrestrial conditions are not possible due to the presence of convective flow in the liquid. 2. Measurements of thermophysical properties of metallic liquids under microgravity conditions, including the specific heat, viscosity, surface tension. These are then compared with measurements made under terrestrial conditions, when possible. These data are important for the analysis of the nucleation data, fluid flow calculations; additionally they are important for understanding fundamental connections between liquid dynamics and structure. Approach Measurements of the supercooling are made under controlled stirring conditions. The nucleation rates are derived from the distribution of maximum supercooling following the approach developed by Skripov. By systematically changing the fluid-flow rates by applying controlled voltages to the heater/positioner coils in the ESA electromagnetic levitation facility on the ISS (ISS-EML), the nucleation rates can be studied as function of stirring, allowing the hypothesis to be examined. Hypothesis When the composition of the nucleating crystal phase is different from that of the liquid, long range diffusion can become important for the nucleation process. The potential impact on the nucleation rate depends on the ratio of the attachment rates to the nucleating cluster and the time required for long-range diffusion. This can be investigated by nucleation measurements made as a function of stirring, since fluid-flow can decrease the impact of long-range diffusion. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Realistic modeling approaches have the potential to greatly improve the efficiency of materials development. However, progress is frequently hampered by the lack of relevant thermophysical property data needed for the modeling. Further, key processes such as crystal nucleation from the liquid or other phase transitions within the liquid, are often poorly understood, making it difficult to realistically model them. In many cases, the experimental data and a deeper understanding of some processes cannot be obtained from terrestrial experiments due to interactions with sample containers at the temperatures of interest. The combination of the containerless based terrestrial methods and the ISS-EML enables these measurements. In addition to their relevance for processes such as additive manufacturing on earth, where nucleation in highly stirred conditions is important, measurements in two different gravitational fields will be important for designing additive manufacturing in space and on the Moon.
