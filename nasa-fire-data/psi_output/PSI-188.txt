The experiments introduced disorder by added colloidal dopants into the samples; these dopants introduce structural frustration into the crystals which, in turn, can transform the crystals into disordered solids (polycrystalline) and glasses with novel mechanical properties. The experiments varied the concentration and character of particle dopants in three-dimensional colloidal crystals and employed direct visualization of an evolving crystal microstructure and dynamics in microgravity to derive fundamental insight into the nature of the transition and the glasses formed. Revised Objective: Before the ACE-T4 experiment on the International Space Station (ISS) was conducted, the master hard disk controlling the Light Microscopy Module (LMM) failed and delayed starting this experiment by many months. This extended delay had a detrimental effect on the samples that resulted in the gelation of the samples before the experiment began. The originally planned experiments were no longer possible due to gelation, but the ACE-T4 experiment based on the changed samples (gels) provided qualitatively different insights into the behavior of 3D colloidal gels over a range of temperatures and temperature gradients. Some effects were visible with LMM and provide an opportunity to study the microstructure, dynamics, and stresses in gels that can lead to microcracks, an important problem that, for example, could affect 3D printer inks. Approach The ACE-T4 experiment used confocal microscopy to observe the gels over a range of particle packing-fractions (temperatures), using pure, monodisperse poly(N -isopropylacrylamide) (pNIPA) particles in one sample, and then mixtures of pNIPAM particles with different amounts of particle dopants in five other samples. All of the samples formed gels. There was no crystallization in any sample . The data obtained permits the study of 3D gels with and without dopants as a function of temperature; the temperature changes vary the particle size and thus properties of the gels. In addition to the revised ACE-T4 experiment, ground-based soft matter research was completed and published. Hypothesis The ACE-T4 experiments were intended to explore the transformation between a crystal and a glass by varying the density and character of particle dopants in three dimensional colloidal crystals. The original hypothesis stated that ''dopants introduce structural frustration into the crystals which, in turn, can transform the crystals into disordered (polycrystalline) solids and glasses with novel mechanical properties. Direct visualization of the evolving crystal microstructure and dynamics in microgravity will provide fundamental insight into the nature of glasses and into processes such as nucleation and melting.'' Research Impacts/Earth Benefits By frustrating their structural order, the transition from ordered (crystal) to disordered (glassy) materials can alter the macroscopic behavior of ordered materials. Moreover, these experiments could also provide fundamental insight into the nature of nucleation, aging, and melting in materials with varying degrees of order. This information could benefit observation and synthesis of new colloidal materials (e.g. g., meta and photonic materials) for many applications ranging from color control to random laser.
