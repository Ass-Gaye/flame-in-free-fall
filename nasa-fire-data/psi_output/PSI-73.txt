The last flight employed pivalic acid (PVA)—a face centered cubic (FCC) organic plastic crystal that solidifies analogous to many non–ferrous metals unlike SCN that solidifies similar to BCC ferrous metal alloys. In comparison to SCN, PVA exhibits a large anisotropy of its solid–melt interfacial energy. The primary objective was to investigate if the anisotropy of solid-melt interfacial energy is indeed a key physical parameter in the subtle selection process of dendritic operating states. Approach The experimental approach was similar to USMP-3 and USMP-4 with the exception that PVA was utilized as the sample material to quantify growth velocity as a function of undercooling. In addition, the spacing between nearest neighbors were also quantified during this USMP-4 investigation. Hypothesis The hypothesis of the IDGE investigations is that as a function of the supercooling there exists an explicit dendritic dynamic operating state defined by dendritic tip velocity, V and the dendrite tip paraboloidal radius of curvature, R. The Peclet number obtained from the Ivantsov solution for each specified supercooling yielded an infinite number of solutions for V and R. Therefore it is hypothesized that a second independent equation or length scale exists, which, when combined with the Ivantsov diffusion solution, will mathematically select the observed unique dynamic operating state. Two of the candidates for the additional crystal growth physics that could provide the second crucial equation are the effects from the solid-melt interfacial energy, the influence of temperature fluctuations in the melt, or both. Aside from the original hypothesis for USMP-2, the USMP-3 also tested the hypothesis that both the magnitude of the microgravity level and the growth angle with respect to the quasi-static microgravity vector would affect the growth velocity. With additional and sufficient data from USMP-3 it was anticipated to construct a three-dimensional reconstruction of the non-parabolic, non-body-of-revolution dendritic tip shape. For the final USMP-4 investigation the use PVA was to test the hypothesis if interfacial anisotropy has any significant effect on growth velocity and if there exist a minimum nearest neighbor distance below which the growth velocity begins to decrease due to overlapping of thermal fields. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits The size, shape, spacing, and orientation of the dendrites in cast metal alloys have a major effect on the strength, ductility and toughness. Nearly all cast-metal alloys used in everyday products, such as automobiles and airplanes, are composed of dendritic microstructures. Metallurgists have long sought to predict and control alloy microstructures. The development of automated, cost effective manufacturing techniques ultimately depends on the precision with which the problem in non-equilibrium pattern formation can be solved. In principle, the intent is to incorporate fundamental understanding of microstructures into computer codes that simultaneously help design materials with made to order properties and optimize their manufacturability and performance. Dendritic growth experiments conducted in the microgravity environment can provide benchmark data to validate such predictive models.
