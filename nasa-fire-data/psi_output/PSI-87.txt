The vehicle for accomplishing this is an investigation, including experiments to be conducted in microgravity and coupled with appropriate analyses, of the heat transfer and vapor bubble dynamics associated with nucleation, bubble growth or collapse, and subsequent motion. Certain effects that can be neglected at normal Earth gravity, such as surface tension and vapor momentum, can become quite significant in microgravity. Momentum imparted to the liquid by the vapor bubble during growth tends to draw the vapor bubble away from the surface, depending on the rate of growth, which in turn is governed by the temperature distribution in the liquid. Thermophoretic forces, arising from the variation of the liquid-vapor surface tension with temperature, on the other hand, tend to move the vapor bubble toward the region of higher temperature. The bubble motion will be governed by which of these two effects prevail. The elements of nucleate boiling, for which research conducted under microgravity would advance the basic understanding, are: 1. Nucleation or onset of boiling. Indications are that both heater surface temperature and temperature distribution in the liquid are necessary to describe nucleation. 2. The dynamic growth of a vapor bubble in the vicinity of the heater surface. This includes the shape as well as motion of the liquid-vapor interface as growth is taking place. These are influenced by the liquid temperature distribution at the initiation of growth. 3. The subsequent behavior of the vapor bubble. This includes the motion, whether departure takes place or not, and the associated heat transfer. Approach The approach encompasses a comprehensive experimental methodology designed to dissect the nuances of the boiling process in an environment where gravity's influence is minimal. This approach aims to isolate and analyze the core mechanisms that drive nucleate pool boiling, offering insights that are obscured under normal Earth gravity conditions. Here is an outline of the investigation's methodological approach: 1. Experimental Setup in Microgravity: a. The investigation utilizes a specially designed experimental apparatus capable of accurately controlling and measuring heat flux, liquid subcooling levels, and temperature distribution within the liquid and at the heater surface. This apparatus is deployed in a microgravity environment provided by space-based platforms to ensure the minimization of gravity's influence on the boiling process. 2. Controlled Heat Flux Application: a. A key component of the approach involves applying a controlled heat flux to a pool of liquid, initiating the boiling process. The heat flux is carefully modulated to explore a range of boiling conditions, from gentle nucleation to vigorous boiling, under varying levels of liquid subcooling. 3. High-Resolution Data Acquisition: a. To capture the dynamics of bubble formation, growth, and departure, the investigation employs high-resolution, high-speed imaging techniques, along with precise temperature and heat flux measurement tools. This data acquisition setup allows for the detailed observation and analysis of bubble behavior and heat transfer phenomena during the boiling process. 4. Parameter Variation: a. The investigation systematically varies key parameters, including the heat flux magnitude, the degree of liquid subcooling, and the pressure within the system, to study their effects on the nucleation process, bubble dynamics, and heat transfer rates. This parameter variation helps in identifying the critical factors that influence nucleate pool boiling under microgravity. 5. Data Analysis and Model Development: a. The collected data undergoes rigorous analysis to elucidate the fundamental mechanisms of nucleate pool boiling, focusing on nucleation criteria, bubble growth rates, and heat transfer characteristics. Insights gained from the analysis are used to develop and refine predictive models that can accurately describe boiling behavior under a variety of conditions. 6. Cross-Comparison with Earth Gravity Data: a. Findings from the microgravity experiments are compared with data obtained from similar experiments conducted under Earth gravity conditions. This cross-comparison aids in isolating the effects of gravity on the boiling process and contributes to the development of generalized models that can predict boiling behavior across different gravitational environments.
