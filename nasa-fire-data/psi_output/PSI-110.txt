In response to variable gravity levels and ambient vibrational environment the Strata-1 experiment aimed to investigate the behavior of regolith simulants specifically chosen to represent models of regolith covering a range of complexity and tailored to inform and improve computational studies. Approach The Strata-1 experiment consisted of four evacuated tubes partially filled with regolith simulants.The simulants, namely, glass beads, glass shards, meteorite, and carbonaceous chondrite, were chosen to represent models of regolith covering a range of complexity and tailored to inform and improve computational studies. The four tubes are regularly imaged while moving in response to the ambient vibrational environment using dedicated cameras. The imagery is then downlinked to the Strata-1 science team about every two months. Analyses performed on the imagery includes evaluating the extent of the segregation of Strata-1 samples and comparing the observations to computational models. After Strata-1's return to Earth, x-ray tomography and optical microscopy will be used to study the post-flight simulant distribution. There two simulation methods that have been proposed for this research are the Soft-sphere Discrete Element Method (SSDEM) and the Contact Dynamics Method (CD). Hypothesis By utilizing the prolonged microgravity and vibrational conditions of ISS as analogues to those on small Solar System bodies, the Strata-1 investigation aims to answer fundamental questions about how regolith evolves on planetary bodies and what roles particle density, shape, and composition play in the evolution of regolith dynamics. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Strata-1 supports the space exploration program by enabling new interpretation of a wide range of NASA missions including but not limited to Stardust, Deep Impact; EPOXI, Near Earth Asteroid Rendezvous (NEAR), OSIRISREx and Hayabusa-1 and -2. The new Strata information on regolith behavior and mechanics directly assists planning for the Asteroid Redirect Mission ARM), the OSIRIS-REx mission, In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) efforts on small bodies, and upcoming NASA missions to comets and asteroids. The Strata-1 study advances our fundamental understanding of the mechanical behavior of loose material. Results may find utility in any application that involves the mixing of dry materials, such as pharmaceutical tablet manufacture, the study of dune migration and dynamics, and similar fields.
