The BCAT-5 investigation had four primary objectives corresponding to specific samples: Phase Seperation (BCAT-5-PhaseSep): to study collapse (phase separation rates that impact product shelf-life). Compete (BCAT-5-Compete): to study the competition between phase separation and crystallization, which is important in the manufacture of plastics and other materials. Seeded Growth (BCAT-5-SeededGrowth): to study the properties of concentrated systems of small particles when 99.8% are identical 0.36 diameter micron spheres and 0.2% are 4.14 microns in diameter (11.5x larger); these seed particles may cause heterogeneous crystal growth. 3D Melt (BCAT-5-3D-Melt): to study the mechanisms of crystal formation and 3D melting using colloidal particles that change size with temperature. Approach BCAT is implemented in a bench-top investigation which is set up on the Maintenance Work Area (MWA) on ISS. The samples are contained within a Fluids Module (FM) which held 10 samples in cuvettes in individual wells. The samples were manually homogenized using a stir bar (a Teflon coated mixing magnet) inside each cuvette. Samples are mixed, examined and imaged using EarthKAM, to document the separation of colloid and liquid components and the formation of colloidal crystals over time. Hypothesis Colloidal particles would exhibit behaviors analogous to atoms, allowing the study of phase transitions, crystallization, and self-organization without the interference of sedimentation and convection present on Earth. This investigation hypothesizes that by taking advantage of microgravity and avoiding sedimentation binary alloys with very different materials and sizes and by addition of polymers particles to induce attractive forces can lead to exciting and promising form of colloidal engineering to synthesize a new class of materials. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Insights from BCAT-5 enhance the design of colloidal products critical to space missions, such as food and consumables. The Phase Separation component investigates multi-phase interactions with potential applications in liquid waste recycling, coolant systems, and rocket fuel flow control. By manipulating crystal size during production, BCAT-5 aims to create stronger, more durable materials with extended shelf lives for both terrestrial and extraterrestrial use. Additionally, BCAT-5 advances colloidal engineering by studying how crystallization interacts with phase separation, a key factor in improving consumer and industrial products. Findings help reduce manufacturing defects in colloid-based materials, like plastics, and optimize household goods, such as fabric softeners, by minimizing costly additives. The experiment also supports breakthroughs in next-generation technologies, including advanced optics, medical tools, and smart materials. The Seeded Growth component examines how larger seed particles influence crystal size, enabling better control in producing ceramics, optical filters, and composite materials while refining predictive computer models for crystal formation.
