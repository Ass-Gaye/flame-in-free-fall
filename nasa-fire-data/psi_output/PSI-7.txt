The work comprises the application of available knowledge to the preparation and characterization of particle stabilized emulsions, produced by adapting to the Soft Matter Dynamics instrument. The role of gravity on quiescent wet foams can be captured in 2 key questions: Is the growth law for average bubble size R~√ time, such that R dR/dt is a constant? If so, what is the liquid-fraction dependence of this rate? How do the rate and the nature of the bubble rearrangement dynamics change as the liquid fraction is increased to the point of un-jamming (transition solid-like to liquid-like)? PASTA will address the SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES: Evaluate the characteristic time for the droplet coalescence as a function of the formulation Identify and investigate specific dynamic regimes for droplets dynamics (for example Brownian vs. Capillarydriven) during emulsion destabilization. Test and develop models for emulsion stability/destabilization prognosis. Approach The PArticle STAbilised emulsions experiment (PASTA) is placed inside the Fluid Science Rack (FSL) in the European Columbus module of the ISS. Each PASTA sample cell is fitted with a piston that allows to agitate the mixture within the cell at various frequencies and durations to emulsify the mixture. The sample cells are grouped 4-by-4 in sample cell units that can be fitted on a carousel inside SMD. This carousel allows for placing each individual sample cell in the diagnostics position of SMD, where it can be observed with the SMD sensors and several diagnostic tools, like the overview camera that takes microscope image sequences in black and white of the sample cell. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Emulsions have large relevance for applications in a wide spectrum of technologies, processes, and products in different fields including oil and gas, specialty materials, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, and foods. Both stable and unstable emulsions are needs that are shared across a variety of applications. For example, efficient de-emulsifiers are needed to facilitate the process of phase separation (breaking) in the crude oil emulsions extracted from the wells, while high stability oil-in-water emulsions are required in the final stage of production of the same well or to facilitate pumping high-viscosity oils through pipelines. In the food industry, many products based on emulsified fats or oils (condiments, sauces, frozen confections, and others) require long term stability. However, destabilizing emulsions are key to processing raw food materials such as milk and vegetable oils. Contact(s) Libero Liggieri,James K Ferriemail,T.
