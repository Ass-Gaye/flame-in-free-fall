Precise measurements of interface shape, cell/dendrite tip radius, tip composition and tip temperature will be carried out as a function of composition, growth rate and thermal gradient values. By systematically varying the process parameters in the absence of gravity-induced convection, the range of interface morphologies will be obtained, yielding insight into the underlying principles that govern dynamical pattern selection. Directional solidification experiments conducted in microgravity using transparent organic analogues will provide real time benchmark data to quantitatively determine conditions for the planar to cellular and cell to dendrite transitions and validate a predictive phase field 3D being developed as part of the investigation. Approach Experiments were performed within the Directional Solidification Insert (DSI) of the Device for the study of Critical LIquids and Crystallization (DECLIC) aboard the International Space Station. Homogeneous 3D cylindrical samples in a classical Bridgman setup was processed with various pulling velocities V and temperature gradients G. The transparent succinonitrile (SCN)-0.24wt% camphor alloy was the sample material and it permitted in situ optical imaging of the solid/liquid interface. Experiments were typically initiated from a single crystal growing along the temperature gradient direction. The alloy solidified within a cylindrical crucible with an inner diameter of 10mm. The solid/liquid interface was located within a stable and well-controlled temperature gradient. The crucible was pulled through an axial adiabatic zone, allowing the interface to grow up to 100 mm in length at a controlled velocity. While independently controlling V and G experienced by the interface, in situ evolution of the solid/liquid interface morphology was imaged and analyzed. 3D phase-field simulations were specifically developed to identify ranges of primary spacings for the formation of the classically observed “fingers” (deep cells) with blunt tips and “needles” with parabolic tips. Hypothesis When a planar solid-liquid becomes unstable, under certain growth conditions, complex interactions among perturbed regions can lead to different amplification rates of different wavelengths that determines the final selection of the reorganized pattern. This complex dynamical process selects a small range of wavelengths, from an unstable spectrum of wavelengths, to form a periodic array of cells. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits From the technological viewpoint, the importance of cellular and dendritic microstructures is quite clear since these structures are exhibited in all important commercial solidification techniques. The proposed project will for the first time provide benchmark data for the dynamical evolution of three-dimensional cellular and dendritic patterns that will enable precise modeling of complex commercial processing technologies to give reliable products. The development of a reliable model will allow industries to design new materials and processing conditions that will have a significant impact on the economy in the US, since casting and welding industries are several billion dollars industries. In addition, the fundamental knowledge of pattern formation will allow the development of nano-scale patterns in polymers and electronic materials that will have significant impact on the development of high technology and advanced materials.
