Approach 3 different Al-Si alloys were directionally solidified under different thermal gradient and furnace translation velocities. Along with micro and macro-segregation, dendritic characteristics including primary arm spacing and trunk diameters were quantified. Microgravity results were compared to those obtained under terrestrial conditions. A finite volume modeling approach was undertaken in the absence of convection and predictions were compared to data obtained from microgravity and terrestrial samples. Hypothesis Dendritic metrics of the directionally solidified (DS) samples are expected to have dendritic characteristics that compare very well to model predictions for growth in the absence of convection. Earth-grown samples are not anticipated to compare well, indicating that the convection in Earth grown alloys does indeed play a role in microstructural development during the solidification process. Further, Earth-grown samples exhibit a type of macrosegregation called ''steepling” in which the periphery of a DS-ed sample is of eutectic composition and is seen in alloys with solutes that increase the density of the liquid during solidification. Samples grown aboard the ISS are not expected to have steepling. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits The research is relevant to a process known as directional solidification, which is used for producing critical castings that are used in gas turbine engines. Gas turbine engines power aircraft (i.e., often called ''jet engines'') and are also used to generate electric power. These castings are made of vacuum melted alloys that are capable of withstanding the demanding conditions at high temperatures and consequently the metallurgical structure of the alloys must be of the highest quality. The research is leading to an improved understanding of how microstructures and segregation form in directionally solidified castings; hence the science of producing such castings will be enhanced as a result of the research. In addition, the research findings relate to many other types of castings, including billets and ingots, of alloys (i.e., steel, aluminum alloys, etc.) that are critical for metal fabrication. In addition, the benchmark microgravity data allows validation of models predicting dendritic metrics such as primary and secondary arm spacing and trunk diameter. Such validated simulation models can enable optimization of casting parameters.
