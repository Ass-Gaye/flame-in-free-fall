Approach The CSLM-2R approach and hardware was identical to the predecessor CSLM-2 investigation. The solid volume fraction of the 4 samples for CSLM-2R were 0.05. 0.10, 0.15, and 0.20 and the coarsening times ranged from 1.6 to 48 hours. Hypothesis The theory of coarsening has been described by Lifshitz, Slyozov and Wagner (LSW) predicts that for the limit of zero volume fraction particles grow with time as the cube root of the average radius, and the particle size distribution becomes independent of time. The investigation hypothesizes that finding a system which satisfies all the constraints of LSW theory such as particles are infinitely separated and there is no diffusional interaction between them is prohibitive for ground-based laboratory condition due to effects of particle sedimentation and buoyancy-induced convection and therefore the theory can only be tested by performing experiments in a microgravity environment. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Coarsening occurs on Earth during the processing of any metal alloy and thus the coarsening process affects a large spectrum of products from dental fillings to turbine blades. Since the properties of an alloy are linked to the size of the particles within the solid, coarsening can be used to strengthen materials. This is the case with the majority of aluminum alloys used commercially today. Conversely, if the coarsening process proceeds too long the material can weaken. This occurs in jet turbine blades and is one of the reasons why turbine blades must be replaced after a certain number of hours of service. Thus developing accurate models of the coarsening process is central to creating a wide range of new materials encompassing those used in automobiles to those used in space applications.
