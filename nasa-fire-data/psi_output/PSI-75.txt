An objective was to study the effect of particle shape on the kinetics of aggregation and structures formed by DC and pulsed magnetic fields in suspensions of superparamagnetic particles. Specifically, the investigation studied the suspensions of paramagnetic ellipsoid-shaped particles and quantify the effect of particle shape on the interactions of dipolar chains and the resulting suspension microstructure. Approach InSPACE-3 employed video microscopy to obtain suspension structural information. Two particle shapes were tested for four frequency conditions and four field strengths, for a total of 24 experimental runs. Each run took approximately one hour. Each sample had a particle volume fraction of 0.52%. This concentration represents an optimum value based on previous microgravity experiments, designed to balance the ability to capture aggregation kinetics at short times in DC fields the structural evolution in pulsed fields such that the final coalescence stage is observed. For detailed experimental approach please refer Science Requirements Document (SRD). Hypothesis Based on the science results of InSPACE-2, InSPACE-3 hypothesizes that particle shape will alter the aggregation kinetics, microstructures and microscopic mechanics, such as buckling instabilities that are observed for suspensions of polarizable spheres. This research will potentially lead to the ability to engineer enhanced properties of these suspensions, including the ability to suppress lateral aggregation in magnetorheological fluid-based electromechanical devices or the ability to create new functional materials that control the flow of electromagnetic and thermal energy. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits The quick phase-shifting property of magnetic colloidal fluids studied in InSPACE-3 makes them potentially useful for devices such as optical interfaces, active noise, and vibration dampers inside spacecrafts. Future uses could include robotics, energy-transfer devices such as clutches, and other control systems and on Earth directed self-assembly of crystalline structures from particles that may eventually allow creation of new nano-materials fabricated from nanoparticle building blocks, with potential applications in medicine, energy storage, chemical separations, and catalysis. Magnetic colloidal fluid technology is currently used for shock absorbers in race cars and sport cars. This technology is expanding to include making large-scale building foundation stabilizers for areas prone to earthquakes. Contact(s) Eric M. Furstemail,Robert D.
