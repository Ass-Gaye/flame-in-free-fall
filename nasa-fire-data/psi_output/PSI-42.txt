Specific interest was in examining dendritic structure close to the dendrite tip. Additional objectives included measuring the rate at which topological singularities occur when a secondary dendrite arm separates from the main dendrite stem or when two arms fuse together. Approach The CSLM-3 approach and hardware was essentially identical to the predecessor CSLM and CSLM-2 investigations. The solid volume fraction of the 4 Pb-Sn samples for CSLM-3 were 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4. Isothermal holding times between 10 minutes and 48 hours were employed. After the requisite holding times the samples were quenched using pressurized gas. Upon receipt of the samples they were analyzed using three-dimensional reconstructions of the microstructure. The 3D reconstructions was used to determine the interfacial curvatures, the genus and the spatial distribution of curvature in the system. The experimentally measured structures were used to compare phase field simulations and experiment. Hypothesis The fundamental questions that this investigation attempts to answer are as follows: Does the Interface Shape Distribution (ISD) vary with position from the tip of the dendrite? If so, as is likely the case, does the ISD scale with time, as is usually the case during coarsening? How important is the spatial distribution of curvature in setting the evolution of the structure? How do diffusional interactions between the secondary, and perhaps tertiary, arms on different dendrites affect the evolution of the system? How does the ISD evolve when there are multiple dendrites in the system? Does scaling exist? Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Coarsening occurs on Earth during the processing of any metal alloy and thus the coarsening process affects a large spectrum of products from dental fillings to turbine blades. Since the properties of an alloy are linked to the size of the particles within the solid, coarsening can be used to strengthen materials. This is the case with the majority of aluminum alloys used commercially today. Conversely, if the coarsening process proceeds too long the material can weaken. This occurs in jet turbine blades and is one of the reasons why turbine blades must be replaced after a certain number of hours of service. This investigation will provide insights into the dynamics of dendritic morphological evolution and will point the way to the formulation of models of this technologically important coarsening process. Thus developing accurate models of the coarsening process and validating them using benchmark experimental data is central to creating a wide range of new materials encompassing those used in automobiles to those used in space applications.
