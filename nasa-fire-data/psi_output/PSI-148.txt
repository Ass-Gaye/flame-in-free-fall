Extract data, such as, fluid velocities along stream lines as a function of distance from the surface-void, angular rotation of the fragmented side arms due to Marangoni flow lines, etc. from PFMI (Pore Formation and Mobility Investigation) videos, and then compare these observations with the simulations in order to quantitatively validate our numerical model. Use that model to predict formation of spurious grains in a totally different alloy, Al-7Si (MICAST), under varying processing conditions, such as, during re-melting prior to DS, after a step increase or decrease in growth speed vs at constant speed, etc. Approach Use the PFMI and MICAST experimental flight data available in PSI to validate a three dimensional solidification model to predict that formation of spurious grains during DS. Hypothesis Columnar and dendritic growth experiments in a predominantly diffusive microgravity environment have shown the presence of voids at the melt-crucible interface which are the likely the source of Marangoni convective flows. It is hypothesized that it is this flow that is responsible for fragmentation of the dendrite-side arm, and its rotation which creates the nucleus for the subsequent growth and formation of spurious grains. This hypothesis was tested by numerically simulating the influence of the Marangoni convection on the trajectory of such a dendrite fragment in the alloy melt and comparing with the experimental observations. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits Results from this research are valuable not only for terrestrial technologies involving solidification, such as repairing gas turbine blade tips by laser cladding, but also for any future space manufacture or repair which involves solidification.
