On the microscopic scale, the scientific objective is to evaluate the local details of the Kelvin-Clapeyron model of change-of-phase heat transfer under microgravity conditions where the extended Young-Laplace equation predominates. Approach The CVB-1 investigation used a variety of methods to study the behavior of the constrained vapor bubble (CVB) in a microgravity environment. This approach involved conducting experiments, creating mathematical models, and using insights from past CVB experiments to analyze the findings after the flight. 1. Experimental Design and Testing: -Customized CVB Cells: Five CVB cells were fabricated from quartz. Each cell featured a square cross-section (3 x 3 mm) and varying lengths to achieve specific liquid-to-vapor volume ratios. One cell was a ''dry'' cell, evacuated to 5 torr, to establish baseline thermal behavior. The remaining four cells were filled with either pentane (a completely wetting fluid) or ethanol (a partially wetting fluid) before launch. Three cells utilized pentane at different volume ratios, while one was intended for ethanol. -Controlled Thermal Environment: The CVB cells were integrated into a system that allowed for precise control of heat input at the evaporator end and heat rejection at the condenser end. The system maintained a stable and uniform thermal environment for the cells. -Microgravity Environment: The CVB experiments were conducted on the International Space Station (ISS) to provide the necessary microgravity conditions. 2. Data Acquisition: -Macroscopic Measurements: Throughout the experiment, macroscopic data were recorded, including temperature profiles along the cell, vapor pressure, heat input and rejection rates, and visual observations of the vapor bubble. These observations were used to assess the overall performance and stability of the CVB system. -Microscopic Measurements: The investigation utilized an Image Analyzing Interferometer (IAI) to acquire detailed, microscopic measurements of the liquid film profile. These measurements included curvature profiles, film thickness profiles, and adsorbed film thickness. These data were crucial for understanding the local transport processes within the CVB. -Acceleration Measurements: The system monitored residual accelerations to assess their potential impact on the CVB's stability and behavior. Time Correlation: All acquired data were meticulously time-correlated to ensure accurate analysis and interpretation. 3. Mathematical Modeling and Analysis: - Extended Young-Laplace Equation: This equation, incorporating both capillary and van der Waals forces, was employed to model the equilibrium shape of the liquid-vapor interface. Kelvin-Clapeyron Transport Model: This model was used to analyze the non-equilibrium behavior of the CVB. It incorporated conservation equations for mass, momentum, and energy, along with the extended Young-Laplace equation. The model was considered for refinement to account for internal radiative heat transfer, observed in previous experiments. -Numerical Modeling: Two- and three-dimensional numerical thermal modeling were considered to evaluate and refine the Kelvin-Clapeyron model, particularly to capture the complex interplay between capillary and Marangoni convection. -Internal Heat Analysis: Temperature data were analyzed to quantify the contributions of conduction, external radiation, and internal radiation to the overall heat transfer within the CVB system. This analysis built upon previous ground-based studies and included a detailed examination of the role of Marangoni convection. 4. Comparison with Ground-Based Studies: -Equilibrium Studies: The microgravity equilibrium data, including film thickness and curvature profiles, were compared with ground-based measurements to confirm the validity of the extended Young-Laplace equation under these conditions. -Non-Equilibrium Studies: Comparison of microgravity non-equilibrium data with results from ground-based studies illuminated the influence of gravity on CVB behavior. Specifically, this comparison focused on the differences in flow field symmetry and the magnitude of heat transfer. 5. Stability Analysis: -Macroscopic Stability: The investigation evaluated the overall stability of the CVB system, considering the occurrence of dryout, oscillations, and cavitation, as a function of heat flux, liquid-to-vapor volume ratio, and working fluid. -Microscopic Stability: A stability analysis was performed, leveraging experimental observations and mathematical modeling, to understand the conditions and mechanisms that lead to instability within the thin liquid film. 6. Post-Flight Analysis: -Reflectivity and Image Data: Detailed analysis of the raw reflectivity and image data was conducted to extract information about the adsorbed film thickness and its influence on CVB behavior. Hypothesis It is hypothesized that the isothermal equilibrium liquid film shape depends on the gravitational field and the intermolecular force field which changes rapidly in the vicinity of the liquid-vapor and liquid-solid interfaces. This equilibrium shape in a particular gravitational field can be controlled by fixing the container shape, the liquid-substrate system, and the relative volume of the liquid to that of the vapor. It is further hypothesized that the extended Young-Laplace equation can describe this shape over a large range of conditions. Under non-equilibrium conditions resulting from an imposed temperature gradient, fluid flow and change-of-phase heat transfer are a function of further changes in the shape of the liquid volume. It is postulated that these non-equilibrium effects can be analyzed using a Kelvin-Clapeyron transport model that includes the use of the conservation of heat, mass and momentum and the extended Young-Laplace equation. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits CVB has performed ground-based studies in a thermal vacuum chamber to determine the efficiency of the heater and cooler configuration. Large thermal response times that have been experimentally observed in space-based experiments cannot be obtained from these ground-based studies. Space-based experimentation is the only method available to ascertain internal low-gravity fluid mechanics within a heat pipe. The project aims to achieve an improved understanding of microscale heat transfer, improved designs for wickless heat pipes, and an increased efficiency in heat transfer devices for cooling critical components. Targeted users are existing microelectronics industry and perhaps military applications. New designs should be able to be developed several months following the analysis and presentation of the results from the experiment. Contact(s) Joel L. Plawskyemail,Peter C.
