Specifically, the objective was to measure the average intercept length and the average section area of the solid particles at various times and for various volume fractions of solid. Using this data the amplitude of the temporal power law for the average section area. and intercept length was to be determined. Once the amplitude of the temporal power law is known, the objective is to compare the result to the theoretical predictions on the dependence of the amplitude of the power law on the volume fraction of the coarsening phase. The objective was also to determine the steady-state particle size distribution by measuring the distribution of section area of of the particles in a plane and the intercept length distribution. Approach The sample holder in the Sample Processing Unit (SPU) contained four Lead-Tin samples of 12 mm diameter and 6 mm thickness for various volume fractions of Tin. The SPU heated the samples up to 185 degree C in less than 9.5 min, held the temperature for a predetermined time at 185 ± 0.1 degree C ranging from 0.25 to 48hrs (coarsening phase). The heating system consists of two disc resistance heaters and one ring heater to insure uniform heating with four resistance temperature devices (RTD) installed mid-way between the discs 1 to measure temperature of the samples. Subsequent to the predetermined coarsening time the samples were quenched using pressurized air to nearly room temperature in approximately 1 minute. Once the samples were returned to earth they were sectioned and the measurements of particle size and distribution was performed. Hypothesis The theory of coarsening has been described by Lifshitz, Slyozov and Wagner (LSW) predicts that for the limit of zero volume fraction particles grow with time as the cube root of the average radius, and the particle size distribution becomes independent of time. The investigation hypothesizes that finding a system which satisfies all the constraints of LSW theory such as particles are infinitely separated and there is no diffusional interaction between them is prohibitive for ground-based laboratory condition due to effects of particle sedimentation and buoyancy-induced convection and therefore the theory can only be tested by performing experiments in a microgravity environment. Research Impacts/Earth Benefits The importance of coarsening from a commercial perspective arises from the major effect that coarsening has on the properties of materials, for example materials can become stronger while undergoing coarsening. The CSLM experiment will assess the validity of the theory of coarsening that has been used to design materials. The result will be a more rational design of materials using a first-principles approach, reduced development cost for the production of new materials and better materials properties. Such a first-principles design of materials will enable NASA and industry to create materials in a rapid cost-efficient manner with the superior properties that are needed for space exploration.
