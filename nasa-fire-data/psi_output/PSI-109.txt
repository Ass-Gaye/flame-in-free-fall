The onset conditions and nature of oscillatory flows. 2. The effect of the heating mode and the static free surface shape on the oscillations. 3. The shape and motion of the free surface during oscillations. 4. The coupling among the free surface deformation, the surface temperature distribution, and the velocity field. Approach The STDCE-2 investigation employed a systematic approach to explain the role of free surface deformation in oscillatory thermocapillary flows under microgravity conditions. This approach comprised: Controlled Variation of Key Parameters: -Container Size: Utilizing three chamber diameters (1.2 cm, 2.0 cm, 3.0 cm) allowed researchers to examine the influence of scale on the onset of oscillations and assess potential inertial effects. -Heating Mode: Employing both submerged heater (CT mode) and CO2 laser (CF mode) systems enabled the comparison of flow behavior under constant temperature difference and constant heat flux boundary conditions, respectively. -Free Surface Shape: Tests were conducted with flat, concave, and convex free surfaces to explore the interplay between surface curvature and flow dynamics. Advanced Diagnostic Techniques: -Flow Visualization: The use of tracer particles enabled the visualization and qualitative characterization of flow patterns, including the three-dimensional, time-dependent structures observed during oscillations. -Infrared Imaging: An infrared scanner captured the temperature distribution across the oil's free surface, providing insights into the thermal gradients driving the thermocapillary flow and their evolution during oscillations. -Optical Deformation Measurement: A Ronchi system was employed to measure the free surface deformation and motions, allowing researchers to quantify the dynamic changes in surface shape and correlate them with observed flow behavior and temperature variations. Interactive Operation: The experiment was conducted interactively, allowing the researchers on the ground to communicate with the astronauts onboard the Spacelab, observe the downlinked video images in real-time, and adjust test parameters based on the observed phenomena. This flexibility facilitated the exploration of unexpected results and the fine-tuning of the experimental conditions to optimize data collection. The STDCE-2 investigation gathered comprehensive data aimed at complex dynamics of oscillatory thermocapillary flows and verifying the hypothesized role of free surface deformation in their onset. Hypothesis The onset of oscillatory thermocapillary flow, particularly in microgravity environments, is significantly influenced by free surface deformation, which acts in conjunction with the Marangoni number (Ma). • Buoyancy masks the true oscillation mechanism in Earth- based experiments: The experimental data on oscillatory thermocapillary flows taken in normal gravity environments are often confusing or contradictory due to the presence of buoyancy. This suggests that buoyancy may obscure the role of free surface deformation in the oscillation process. • Marangoni number alone is insufficient to predict oscillations in microgravity: The unexpected stability observed in the prior STDCE microgravity experiments, even at Marangoni numbers exceeding the critical threshold observed in ground-based tests, indicates that Ma is not the sole determinant of oscillatory onset in the absence of buoyancy. • Free surface deformation is a key factor: The interaction between thermocapillary forces and free surface deformation plays a key role in the oscillation mechanism.
